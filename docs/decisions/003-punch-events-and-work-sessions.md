# ADR 003: Attendance batches, punches and work sessions

- Status: Accepted; implemented as per the ERD of 2026-11-06
- Date: 2026-11-06

## Context

Attendance must stay individually identifiable for every employee, including
when a LeadHand clocks in a group, and for employees who have no app account.
Punches can arrive late or twice because of offline sync. Payroll needs
auditable inputs.

## Decision

Four tables, all in `apps/timekeeping`:

1. **`AttendanceBatch`**: one clock-in or clock-out action performed on a
   device by a user (the actor). It records:
   - the job and action type
   - the device time (`captured_at`) and the server time (`received_at`)
   - the location assessment (ADR 004)
   - the source
   - the client-generated **`idempotency_key`**, which is unique (ADR 002)

   An individual punch is a batch of one.
2. **`AttendanceBatchItem`**: the outcome for **one employee** in a batch.
   It is accepted or rejected with a message, and records the
   `JobAssignment` it was validated against.
3. **`TimePunch`**: generated by an accepted item, at most one per item. It
   is immutable. Its instant is the batch's `captured_at`.
4. **`WorkSession`**: one employee's attendance on one job, from clock-in to
   clock-out. It is authorised by a `JobAssignment`, and holds at most one
   clock-in and one clock-out punch. `work_date` comes from the company
   timezone (ADR 008). Review status and reviewer live here.

The **subject** of attendance is always an `Employee`, through the item and
the assignment. The **actor** is the `User` who performed the batch.

## Consequences

- A group clock-in creates one item, one punch and one session per employee.
  It never creates a shared session (ADR 006).
- The database guarantees:
  - one item per employee per batch
  - one punch per item
  - one clock-in and one clock-out per session
  - one open session per assignment

  Services enforce the rules that span assignments, such as never being
  clocked in to two jobs at once.
- Batches, items and punches are never edited. The design for corrections is
  deferred (see `docs/development/data-model.md`).
- Payroll consumes closed (and, where required, approved) sessions.

## Update (2026-11-10): moving between job sites

- An employee may have several completed sessions on the same work date,
  across different jobs, and more than one on the same job when they return.
- They never have more than one **open** session. The attendance service
  enforces this across all assignments, under a lock on the employee row
  (`services/sessions.py`). The database only enforces one open session per
  assignment; a database-level per-employee rule is deferred (Q15).
- A clock-out always closes the open session against its original
  assignment. Moving between jobs never modifies assignments.
- An ended assignment still covers offline punches that were captured before
  it was ended and are dated on or before its end date (Q6). Its
  `updated_at` marks the moment it ended, until a dedicated column exists
  (Q11, deferred).
- Offline events with identical device times are processed with clock-outs
  first (Q13).
